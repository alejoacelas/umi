The pane strip

Option C from Where panes live, chosen for showing hidden panes and for bringing them back: every pane of a tab gets a chip in a strip, bright when on show, dim when hidden. Three questions, one per section. In each section only that question changes; everything else stays at the stated default. Content is made up; syntax is illustrative. Focus and "needs you" are drawn as Umi draws them today (the cyan line, an amber dot); the single per-pane indicator that may replace them is explored in The pane indicator. Also: Resurfacing · The drawer · Moving panes from the keyboard.

1. The order of the chips

The same five steps play in all four windows at once. Watch which chips move, and whether you could find a pane by its position.

Held: one strip across the tab · a returning pane takes a free place · tab website with five panes, three on show.

Start: redesign, projects.md and dev server on show; image-resize and deploy-check hidden. Focus in redesign.

2. Where the strip sits

The same tab in each: a Claude pane, a Markdown file with its header, and a place holding a stack of two notes, with the cyan line on the focused file. Three panes are hidden; deploy-check needs you. The grey number in each window's corner is how much chrome sits above the first line of text in the file pane.

Held: spatial order (on-show panes left to right, then hidden ones) · ⌘[ ⌘] unchanged.

3. ⌘[ and ⌘] with hidden panes

Use the real keys on this page. The browser takes ⌘[, so plain [ and ] stand in for it. Today ⌘[ ⌘] walk a stack's members, or the tab's panes when the pane isn't stacked.

Held: spatial order · one strip across the tab · a tab shows at most three panes; bringing one in hides the pane used least recently.
